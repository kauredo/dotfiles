# Mockups and guide

Flat vector scenes, not photo composites. Each one's job is to show the mark's geometry doing a job in the world, not the logo stamped on an object. Every rule below is something three blind critics asked for, or something they marked down. Flat scenes cap scores around 6-7: on both runs the critics' last gap was "diagrams, no material, no real setting".

## Everywhere

- Centre on the ink's **bounding box**. Centring on the ink's centroid shifted the d-casa mark away from its heavy stems, and all three critics read it as off-centre.
- Corners follow the mark: a mark of square ends and mitres gets square panels. A rounded panel read as "an app banner".
- Never let the mark touch an edge unless the crop is the idea; then crop big and keep the distinctive part (cropping off the d's floor left "two bars and a chevron").
- An **edge rule** in the mark's geometry: a line mark's own lines (d-casa: two lines 1u apart); a solid mark's silhouette, cropped to a band.

## Business card (350 x 200, front and back)

- Front: primary lockup top left, then name, role, phone and email on one left edge, and a licence number where the business holds one (an estate agency in Portugal has an AMI number; software for agents does not).
- Back: a field in a dark tint, with whole copies cleared around one centred mark, and no plate behind the mark (a plate read as "a sticker over a busy field"). For a line mark, field the mark without its distinctive part (houses without the d), so the centred mark is the only whole one. Keep any part the guide forbids changing: Casas without its door filled the door, which the guide's own rule forbids.

## Tote (400 x 500)

- The mark at poster scale, base visible above the hem.
- A **line mark's** lines may become the bag's parts: the d-casa stems leave the bag's top and fold at the mark's angle into the handle (7.5-8, against 6-6.5 for the mark cropped on a plain bag). A **solid mark** grows nothing: a handle extended from the Casas silhouette read phallic to 3 of 3 critics. Give it plain handles and let scale and crop do the work.

## Street sign (440 x 420)

- A blade sign hung from a bracket in the mark's geometry. A bare bracket arm read as a gallows to one critic; give it the mark's angle or lines.
- The name always appears: a new brand's mark alone tells the street nothing. Mark first and large, the name below at about 80% of the panel width, at least 22 units of margin each side. A name running to the edges lost to a smaller one.

## Avatar (400 x 400, circle)

- The mark only, at about half the circle's height, centred on its ink box. Check it at 40px; a smaller mark "turned to mush at 32px" and lost 0-3.

## One-page guide (1600 x 1040, 12 columns)

- One line on the idea, in the brand's words (d-casa: "Two lines, two agents, one house").
- Construction: the grid the mark was built on, with 1u drawn as a labelled dimension on the mark. A grid finer than 1u labelled "1u" confused every critic.
- Lockups, including the reversed one, each labelled.
- Clear space as a dashed box 2u out from the ink, and a minimum-size row on one baseline at the size where the gaps still open. Render that row and look: the floor stated has to be one the sample on the page meets.
- Colour as a bar in its usage ratio, each label in its own column below (labels under a 10% swatch run together).
- Type: the drawn name, plus one text typeface for everything else, named with its weights.
- Three don'ts people actually try: stretching, a low-contrast pairing from the palette, a drop shadow.
