# Page anatomy by job

Read at Phase 2's Shape step, for the one job the page serves. The job sets where the action sits, which sections belong to the category, and how the page ends. The direction dresses this anatomy and decides its order. Adapted from Hallmark's `references/anatomy.md` (`~/.claude/skills/hallmark/`), which drew it from about fifty real independent sites.

## Every job

- The nav carries the job's own action word: Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free.
- The hero shows the thing itself: the room, the cover, the dates, the product's screen.
- Proof comes in the category's own form: a menu, a lineup, a client list, a changelog. A band of numbers is proof only when someone gave the numbers.
- The recommended plan is the owner's call, with their reason. When the brief lists plans without a pick, ask.

## Content nobody supplied

Build a section the brief gave no content for as a **sample**: an example issue with its parts, dishes typical of the cuisine, three candles named plainly, placeholder track titles. List each sample in the handoff for the owner to replace. A sample shows the shape of the real thing and states no fact a visitor could take as true, so it never carries a price, a date, a person's name, a count or a review. When a section would be nothing but facts nobody gave (a lineup, a tour), make it one line with a slot for the owner, or leave it out.

## subscribe: a newsletter, a waitlist, a podcast

- **Action:** in the hero, and often the hero is the action: the name, one line, the email field. A podcast puts the latest episode and its play control under it.
- **Sections:** a sample or latest issue, set the way it arrives; who writes it; the archive as a dated list of issues or episodes; the reader count only when given, set like a circulation figure.
- **Ends:** a second field under the archive, or the archive itself. This is the one job whose close may be an email field.

## book: a restaurant, a class, a clinic, a studio

- **Action:** a booking picker (party, date, time) opened from the nav and the hero, or the next free times as buttons. Repeat it beside the menu or the classes, never only at the foot.
- **Sections:** the room or the food as a photograph with little or no heading; the menu, by service or as rows with prices; a timetable for classes; how to get there; hours and address as a section of their own.
- **Ends:** hours, address and the booking again. A fine room can carry almost nothing else: one photograph, the menu, the booking.

## visit: a shop, a gallery, a venue, a place

- **Action:** today's hours and the address, in the nav or the hero. Tickets or donations only when the brief has them.
- **Sections:** what is on now as the lead picture; what is coming as dated rows; for a shop, its own things on the shelf; getting there.
- **Ends:** visit information and the address, or the mailing list for what is on next.

## attend: an event, a festival, a race, a conference

- **Action:** tickets in the nav from the first screen. The date and the place are the headline.
- **Sections:** the lineup or the speakers, names set large; the days as a schedule; ticket tiers with prices; the venue and travel; questions. Figures (stages, acts, miles) only when given.
- **Ends:** tickets again, the questions, or the mailing list for the next edition. Between editions the whole page is the date, the place and the list.

## buy: a physical product, a small shop

- **Action:** the product with its price and options, often as the hero; a buy action after the sections that earn it, or a sticky bar with the price.
- **Sections:** the range as tiles with prices; the product in use, close up; how it is made; delivery and the guarantee as plain facts; reviews only when given.
- **Ends:** the offer again, new arrivals, or the guarantee. The email field goes in the footer.

## try: an app, a game, a desktop tool

- **Action:** the store or download buttons right under the headline, one per platform the brief names.
- **Sections:** the key art, trailer or main screen captured from the real product; features as named moments shown in screens, never icon tiles; a before and after where the product changes something; platforms.
- **Ends:** the download again beside one last screen. Games run short: five or six blocks is a full page.

## sign up: software, an API, a developer tool

- **Action:** start free in the nav and the hero, its terms in one line under it. For a developer tool the install command can be the action.
- **Sections:** the product at work, captured or built; what it does as named moments; a code sample or the integration; pricing with its period switch; questions.
- **Ends:** the hero's action again in a short band, or the code sample.

## hire: a studio, a freelancer, an architect

- **Action:** a plain contact (an email, a phone, "Talk to us") in the nav and once more after the work. No form needed.
- **Sections:** the work is the page: a list of projects (name, client, year, place) with pictures, one opened as a case; what the studio does as a short list; where it is.
- **Ends:** contact details, per office when there are several.

## join: a club, a community, a membership

- **Action:** join, or "your first visit", in the hero.
- **Sections:** what a session is like, shown; when and where as a schedule or a list of places; membership options as rows with prices; first-visit guidance; people only in the owner's own pictures.
- **Ends:** the first visit, the membership options, or a sign-up field.

## listen: a band, a film, a podcast, a publication

- **Action:** the new release or current issue with its listen, watch or order buttons side by side.
- **Sections:** the record, film or issue (its cover, its tracks or contents as a list); dates (a tour, screenings) as rows with ticket links; earlier work as covers; videos.
- **Ends:** the dates, more of the catalogue, or the mailing list. Artist pages can be very short: one picture, the release, the dates. With no release in the brief, the act leads (the players, how it sounds in plain words, where it plays) and the page's job becomes booking them, so it closes on the enquiry.

## A job not listed here

Take the nearest job above and adapt its three lines (action, sections, ends) to this one, saying which job you started from: accepting an invite to a product is *sign up* with the inviter named in the hero; a donation page is *join* with the gift as the membership. When no job above is near, find three real independent sites that do the same job, write the three lines from them, and put the URLs in the Shape notes.

## How pages end

Real sites close with these, most common first: the hero's action again in a short band; practical details (hours, address, contacts); more of the catalogue; a short trust tail (a guarantee, press, awards the brief gives); a mailing list, for things that recur. The mailing list is about one close in five.
